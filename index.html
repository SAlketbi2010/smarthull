Fixed-stop repeat tests · 3 November 2026

Nine presses against fixed stops at 8.5, 18.5 and 28.5 mm, in a dark room. A straight line fits the nine presses with R² = 0.96 and RMSE = 0.18 percentage points (about 0.11 pp per mm); repeat presses at one stop agree to a standard deviation of 0.10 percentage points. A one-number linear calibration (about 1,917 µε per 1% decrease) estimates calculated strain with a leave-one-out error of about ±380 µε.

Limits: only two presses at 18.5 and 28.5 mm, no stop below about 1,940 µε, and the strain is calculated from deflection rather than measured with a strain gauge.

MONITOR

Measure a change in light.

A low-cost polymer optical fiber responds to bending. Separate sensing and reference channels record a normalized receiver response. Because the modules use separate emitters, drift cancellation is only partial: over 43 quiet minutes the ratio drifted at a median of 0.055 %/min, against 0.093 %/min for the sensing channel alone.

DETECT

Estimate the strain.

Compare optical measurements with a mechanical reference. The current estimate is a linear calibration; Phase 2 will test whether a machine-learning model trained on more heights and repeats can beat it.

ALERT

Flag a defined condition.

The live page shows a green, amber or red warning against an example 0.2% hull-laminate strain limit. Validated thresholds and an embedded ESP32 estimator are planned for Phase 2.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Make structural monitoring
more accessible.

Strain monitoring can help researchers understand how structures respond to loads. Precise fiber-optic systems such as Fiber Bragg Grating sensors often require specialized interrogation equipment.

SmartHull investigates a simpler intensity-based approach using PMMA polymer optical fiber, photodetection, and lightweight computation. The project will test its accuracy and limitations under controlled conditions.

OUR RESEARCH QUESTION

Can a low-cost optical sensor provide repeatable estimates of bending strain?

Five separate-channel plate trials gave normalized receiver-voltage decreases of 1.19–4.00%. The all-trial linear fit has R² = 0.764 and RMSE = 0.476 percentage points. The supplementary three-trial fit has R² = 0.791 and RMSE = 0.368 percentage points.

THE EXPERIMENT

A clear path from
loading to evidence.

  1. Build and load the specimen.

    A 297 × 210 × 2 mm polycarbonate plate spanned approximately 228.6 mm. Manual bends targeted 10, 20 and 30 mm centre displacement.

  2. Record both optical channels.

    Separate C1 taps fed ESP32 D35 (sensing) and D34 (reference), with D25 driving both TX inputs. Record a fresh baseline, bend and release.

  3. Compare the alternatives.

    Retain historical fiber-only and single-channel results alongside the newer reference-channel trials. Report movement observations and both linear fits.

  4. Test on unseen cycles.

    In Phase 2, separate entire cycles for training, validation and testing; assess repeatability, recovery and ESP32 inference performance.
