MONITOR

Measure a change in light.

A low-cost polymer optical fiber responds to bending. Two detection channels help separate the sensing response from shared source drift.

DETECT

Estimate the strain.

Compare optical measurements with a mechanical reference, then test whether machine learning improves the estimate.

ALERT

Flag a defined condition.

A future ESP32 prototype would issue an alert when a chosen strain threshold is crossed. The simulation demonstrates this behavior.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Make structural monitoring
more accessible.

Strain monitoring can help researchers understand how structures respond to loads. Precise fiber-optic systems such as Fiber Bragg Grating sensors often require specialized interrogation equipment.

SmartHull investigates a simpler intensity-based approach using PMMA polymer optical fiber, photodetection, and lightweight computation. The project will test its accuracy and limitations under controlled conditions.

OUR RESEARCH QUESTION

Can a low-cost optical sensor provide repeatable estimates of bending strain?

We will test whether a reference channel improves signal stability and whether nonlinear models improve on a linear regression baseline.

THE EXPERIMENT

A clear path from
loading to evidence.

  1. Build and load the specimen.

    Bond a sensing fiber to polycarbonate and apply center loads using a three-point bending rig.

  2. Record both optical channels.

    Collect repeated loading and unloading cycles, paired with calculated reference strain.

  3. Compare the alternatives.

    Evaluate sensing-only versus normalized signals, then linear and nonlinear estimation models.

  4. Test on unseen cycles.

    Separate entire cycles for training, validation, and testing. Report errors, repeatability, hysteresis, and inference speed.
