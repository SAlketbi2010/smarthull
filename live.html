The estimate is a one-number linear calibration fitted to nine fixed-stop presses at 8.5, 18.5 and 28.5 mm (about 1,917 µε per 1% decrease, typical error ±380 µε). No stop was tested below 1,940 µε, so the green zone and the alert levels demonstrate the alert logic rather than a validated safe range. Green is below 1,600 µε, amber 1,600–2,000 µε and red 2,000 µε or more. The levels are fixed from the published hull limit below. Colours are paired with a symbol and a word. A change of level is shown only after it has lasted one second, so a single noisy reading does not trigger an alert.

Where the warning levels come from
  1. Hull limit. Glass-fibre/polyester hull laminates start to micro-crack at about 0.2% strain, after which they take in water and lose stiffness (SP Systems, Guide to Composites). For comparison, BS 4994:1987 (a standard for reinforced-plastic tanks and vessels, not hulls) uses the smaller of 0.1·εR and 0.2% (summarised in Plymouth MATS347 notes). We use 0.2% = 2,000 µε as an example threshold for the red level; a real hull would use its own classification rules and material data.
  2. Approaching. 80% of the limit: 0.8 × 2,000 = 1,600 µε. This margin is our choice, not a code value.
  3. Link to the polycarbonate plate. Strain is a ratio of lengths, so the same value applies in any material. For a plate of thickness t = 2 mm on a span L = 228.6 mm with a central load, ε = 6·t·δ / L². The red level therefore equals a centre deflection δ = ε·L² / (6·t) = 2,000×10-6 × 228.6² / 12 ≈ 8.7 mm, and the amber level about 7.0 mm.
  4. Plate safety. Stress in the plate is σ = E·ε. With E = 2,300 MPa, 2,000 µε gives about 4.6 MPa. Polycarbonate yields at about 60 MPa and 6% strain (data sheet), so the plate is at about 3% of its own yield strain at the red level. The plate is only a curvature stand-in: it is far less stiff than fibreglass and can bend well past 0.2% without damage, so the colours show what the same strain would mean for a GRP hull, not a limit of the plate.
  5. Receiver reading. The estimator maps decrease to strain, so red corresponds to a receiver decrease of 2,000 / 1,917 ≈ 1.04%.

The ±380 µε estimator error is about 19% of the red level, so readings near a boundary can fall on either side. These levels are a demonstration for a polyester/glass hull, not a classification-society approval; other hull materials have different limits.
