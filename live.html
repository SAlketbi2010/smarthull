The estimate is a regression model learned from fixed-stop presses (about 1,917 µε per 1% decrease, typical error ±380 µε). Green is below 1,600 µε, amber 1,600–2,000 µε and red 2,000 µε or more. The levels are fixed from the published hull limit below. Colours are paired with a symbol and a word.

Where the warning levels come from
  1. Hull limit. Glass-fibre/polyester hull laminates start to micro-crack at about 0.2% strain, after which they take in water and lose stiffness (SP Systems, Guide to Composites). The British Standard for reinforced-plastic structures limits allowable strain to the smaller of 0.1·εR and 0.2% (BS 4994:1987, summarised in Plymouth MATS347 notes). So the red level is εlimit = 0.2% = 2,000 µε.
  2. Approaching. 80% of the limit: 0.8 × 2,000 = 1,600 µε. This margin is our choice, not a code value.
  3. Link to the polycarbonate plate. Strain is a ratio of lengths, so the same value applies in any material. For a plate of thickness t = 2 mm on a span L = 228.6 mm with a central load, ε = 6·t·δ / L². The red level therefore equals a centre deflection δ = ε·L² / (6·t) = 2,000×10-6 × 228.6² / 12 ≈ 8.7 mm, and the amber level about 7.0 mm.
  4. Plate safety. Stress in the plate is σ = E·ε. With E = 2,300 MPa, 2,000 µε gives about 4.6 MPa. Polycarbonate yields at about 60 MPa and 6% strain (data sheet), so the plate is at about 3% of its own yield strain at the red level. The colours therefore show what the same strain would mean for a GRP hull, not a limit of the plate.
  5. Receiver reading. The estimator maps decrease to strain, so red corresponds to a receiver decrease of 2,000 / 1,917 ≈ 1.04%.

The ±380 µε estimator error is about 19% of the red level, so readings near a boundary can fall on either side. These levels are a demonstration for a polyester/glass hull, not a classification-society approval; other hull materials have different limits.
